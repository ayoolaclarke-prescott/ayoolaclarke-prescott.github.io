The Core Concept

What is the
Governance-Psychology Gap?

"The Governance-Psychology Gap is the space between an organisation's formal data governance framework and the human culture that determines whether that framework is actually followed. It is the distance between what the policy document says and what employees actually do when no one is watching."

Every data breach has a human moment. Someone made a wrong decision. Someone did not understand the protocol. Someone felt that raising a concern was too risky, or simply not worth the effort. These are not IT failures. They are behavioural failures , and they occur in organisations where the culture around data handling has never been deliberately constructed.

Closing the gap requires four disciplines working simultaneously , not sequentially, not separately.

01
Behavioural Economics
People make governance decisions under cognitive load, social pressure, and time constraints. Protocols must be designed around how people actually behave.
02
Organisational Psychology
Team dynamics, leadership style, and psychological safety directly determine whether staff raise concerns or conceal errors. Culture is a governance control.
03
Managerial Psychology
Leaders' own cognitive biases shape hiring, performance management, and incident response. Evidence-based management reduces the human error premium.
04
Information Science
Classification systems, access controls, and records management architecture determine how effectively governance policy translates into operational practice.
